# Mission 04: A service account goes rogue

First mission of the blue specialisation, in two parts. **Part 1:** a service account (normally used only to run a background service, never to sign in like a person) was used on my own workstation inside a window my coach gave me — as if someone had taken it over. Job: reconstruct from my own Sentinel what it did, what it took, and whether it's still in. Stage 0 (how the account's password was actually stolen — Kerberoasting, against the domain controller) happened off my workstation and is reasoned from the mission brief, not observed; my Sentinel doesn't watch the DC yet. **Part 2:** I perform that same Kerberoasting attack myself — as an ordinary domain user, against my own seat's service account only — to close the loop from "reasoned about" to "reproduced hands-on," with the rules of engagement explicitly forbidding touching any other seat's account even though enumeration makes all of them visible.

**Status: done — all nine steps complete across both parts, all four CTFd flags submitted.** Part 1: `svc-app58` was Kerberoasted (reasoned, not observed — happens against the DC), then used to run a recon burst on WKS-L58, stage a small customer-data cache, and plant a 15-minute recurring scheduled task for persistence — still active at the time of investigation. "Recon" (50 pts, the account's SPN service class, `MSSQLSvc`), "Data staged" (100 pts), and "Persistence" (150 pts) all solved. Part 2: enumerated SPN accounts from my own laptop, requested a TGS ticket for my own seat's account only, cracked it offline in under a second against `rockyou.txt` (via WSL after native Windows blocked hashcat with an Application Control policy), signed onto WKS-L58 as the service account, and in Sentinel compared my own footprint against the original intruder's and confirmed why the ticket-request step itself is invisible to this workspace. "Kerberoast" (200 pts) solved.
